# Changelog

## 1.0.0 (unreleased)
The mod has not been released yet, so its version is 1.0.0 and stays 1.0.0 until the user says otherwise (`_agent/PROJECT_RULES.md`): new changes are added below under this heading, `Plugin.ModVersion` and `manifest.json` are not bumped. The 0.x headings further down are the development history from before the first release.

- Water splash timing (user: about 0.3 s late): the stream was followed down to the ground, which in a filled pool is the pool floor. It now stops at the liquid surface (pump pool, tar pit, sea / lake) when that is higher, so the splash sound and the splash particles come when and where the water really hits. New `[Effects] WaterSplashOffset` (s, default 0) for fine tuning. Built, NOT tested in game.
- Pump stroke sound in three parts (user): a scratch while the piston rises (Push / first 42 % of a Cycle loop), a scratch while it falls (Pull / rest of the loop) and the pump_up hit placed so its loudest moment lands when the piston reaches the bottom. The scratches are the two halves of the user's `pump-scratching` recording (cut at 0.98 s), fitted to their part of the stroke (speed 0.85 - 1.25, faded at the end). `[Effects] ScratchSounds` (default on; off = the old single recording).
- Water splash (user): the recorded `water-splash` plays where the pumped water comes down, when the first droplets land (softer for a small stroke). `[Effects] WaterSplashSound` (default on). Tar keeps its oil_pumped sound at the nozzle. New sounds are embedded in the DLL (`audio/`; originals in `audio/source/`). Built, NOT tested in game.
- Pump sound echo: the game's SFX mixer group adds a strong reverb outdoors (MasterMixer Default snapshot: Reverb +293 mB, decay 0.5 s), which showed up in an OBS recording as a quieter copy of every lever hit about 0.3 s later. New `[Effects] DrySound` (default on) plays the pump sounds through the game's Effects group (no reverb), with the game's sound-effects volume (SfxVol) applied to the sound itself and world reverb zones bypassed. Built, NOT tested in game.
- Pump model: the barrel of the pump body is iron all the way round (9 of its 12 side faces were wood). Blender model changed, FBX re-exported (mesh names, vertex counts and prefab links unchanged), bundle rebuilt. Built, NOT tested in game.
- Bug fixes (docs/BUGS.md #2, #3, #6 - #10): the first pour into a freshly spawned pool waits (up to 3 s) until the pool can receive it instead of being lost; an error in the hose flow effect switches it off for the session only (it used to write TarFlow = false into the config); a pour can match its slug until the slug reached the nozzle plus 1.5 s (laggy hosts); the per-player rate limit tables are pruned; the ground ray under the nozzle no longer allocates; missed-ray ground heights are sampled before the pool lock is taken; console `dtp spawn` from the main menu and pools without depth data no longer throw. Debug lines for all of it. Built, NOT tested in game.
- Debug tool: console command `dtp meshdump [radius]` (default 6 m) writes the pump pool you look at to `DrainTarPit.MeshDump.txt`: per grid point the ground, liquid depth, wall top, the game's vertex height and the height this mod draws, plus a map of walls / liquid and a map of drawn-minus-level in cm in the log. Built, NOT tested in game.
- Log: the same line written again within 2.5 s is skipped (errors are always written). Built, NOT tested in game.
- Rename (user): every "TPD" (the mod's earlier name) is now "DTP": world-save keys `DTP_pool`, `DTP_liquid`, `DTP_baseline`, `DTP_refillT`, `DTP_runoff`. Worlds saved before still load: the old `TPD_` keys are read when the new ones are missing and the new keys are written from then on (`src/Drain/ZdoKeys.cs`). Console help and build comments say DrainTarPit. Built, NOT tested in game.
- Pool edges at a full pool: the surface carried into the walls stays 5 cm under the wall top (it lay on the wall top when the pool was full to the rim: jagged teeth). Built, NOT tested in game.
- Pool edges: the liquid surface runs on level into the wall and ends hidden inside it (it used to slope down to the lowest nearby surface: saw-teeth that caught the sun and flickered). Built, NOT tested in game.
- Tar pool edges: new tar pools use 0.25 m cells like water (`DTP_TarPool3`; older tar pools keep loading), so the edge along a wall steps half as much, and the liquid is drawn fully opaque up to the walls (the game faded the last cell, a see-through band along every wall). Built, NOT tested in game.
- Water editor (F8): new button "Empty pool at crosshair" empties the pump pool (tar or water) you look at; vanilla tar pits are never touched. Built, NOT tested in game.
- Tar pools: new tar pools cover 24 m (48x48 cells of 0.5 m, was 16 m) so a walled tar pool is not cut off by the edge of its area (the tar ran out at the grid border instead of rising to the wall top); pools saved by older versions keep loading at 16 m. The look fix of water pools (no liquid drawn up the walls) now applies to tar pump pools too. A vanilla tar pit takes pumped tar only within 16 m of its centre. Built, NOT tested in game.
- Tar into a walled hole near a vanilla tar pit: the pit's large area used to take the tar (walls do not count there), so it stopped at the edge of the ground. A vanilla pit now takes tar only when you pour into its tar; anywhere else a pump tar pool takes it and walls hold it like water. Built, NOT tested in game.
- Pool look: no sheet on the outer face of tall walls any more (a dry point away from the water was placed 1 m under the TOP of the wall it stands under); every dry point now sits under the lowest ground of the pool's area, and the water surface ends exactly at the inner face of the wall. Built, NOT tested in game.
- Runoff pools clean up after themselves (D-030): a pool that only the runoff made removes itself when it still holds less than 1 m3 a minute after the last liquid reached it, so no small leftover pools pile up along slopes and shores. Pools you pour into yourself are never removed. Built, NOT tested in game.

- Pool look: liquid thinner than 5 cm is no longer drawn (the game draws every wet point at least 10 cm thick, so films of a few millimetres looked like sheets of water on slopes and walls) and no longer counts for the water plane of the wall fix. Built, NOT tested in game.
- Runoff (D-029): overflow runs down to the lowest pool it reaches within 20 m of where it was poured and collects there; further away it vanishes at the ground, it never flows back towards the pool it came from (two pools on a slope used to pass it back and forth until the 6-pool limit and then delete it), and it ends where it reaches the sea or a river. New `src/Drain/RunoffRules.cs`; the deposit RPC carries the stroke origin. Built, NOT tested in game.
- Pool look: no more translucent "curtain" on the outside faces of the pool walls when no water is outside (the water mesh stepped down the outer face to the ground; dry points outside a barrier are now sunk under the ground, the step happens inside the wall). The user's saved look (bluer `_ColorTop` / `_ColorBottom`) is in `docs/DrainTarPit.WaterLook.txt`. Built, NOT tested in game.
- Rim switch off (`PoolRimDisappear = false`): liquid poured into a FULL pool ran over the rim and was then deleted as a "thin film" as soon as the first ground outside the rim was thin, even while it was still flowing downhill (in game: 100 of 100 m3 per stroke). Now only a film that comes to rest outside the rim is dropped; liquid still flowing goes on to outside basins, the grid edge and the next pool (runoff). 4 new unit tests for a full pool (slope, moat outside, small spill on flat ground, rim switch on). Built, NOT tested in game.
- Deep pump pools: a character standing more than about 3 m under the surface did not swim (the game's liquid trigger is a sheet at the surface, read from the game IL; a body fully below it is never entered). Characters inside a pump pool's grid whose feet are under the surface are now entered into the pool with the game's own enter. Built, NOT tested in game.

Enters and exits are now counted per collider (an exit of a collider that was already released is skipped), a counter below 0 is brought back to 0, and a destroyed liquid surface releases every collider it counted. `dtp pits` and the suite print the counters. Built, NOT tested in game.

## 0.8.0
- New: water. A strainer is locked to what its tip sits in when it is built - tar or water - and its whole pipe system only ever pumps that (`DtpPiece.Liquid`, ZDO key `DTP_liquid`, `Liquids.Detect`). A strainer that is built in neither (dry ground), or comes from an older world whose pit is not loaded yet, stays unlocked and locks on its first real stroke (never from the hover text). Once locked it does not change: a tar strainer never pumps water and the other way round.
- Water source: Valheim has one global water plane (`ZoneSystem.m_waterLevel`, 30 m) over the terrain, so there is no volume to lower. A tip counts as 'in water' when `waterLevel - terrain height` at the tip is at least `[Drain] MinWaterDepth` (default 0.3 m) and the tip hangs at most `StrainerReach` above the surface. The supply never runs dry; each stroke moves `VolumePerStroke`. The nozzle must not be in water (`dtp_err_nozzle_in_water`), like the tar nozzle check.
- Water output (this version): the stream, splashes and puddle at the nozzle are bluish, the slug in the hoses is light blue (`TarFlow.Stroke(.., liquid)`, `PumpFx.PourWhenArrives(.., liquid)`; the pump's ZDO carries `DTP_pumpLiquid` so other clients tint the same way), the sound is the synthesised splash. The water is NOT deposited anywhere yet: it pours out and runs away.
- New config `[Drain] AllowWater` (default true), `[Drain] MinWaterDepth`. Hover text says what the pump will move (`Ready to pump water / tar`). EN + DE texts, piece descriptions updated.
- Version 0.8.0 (new minor: everyone on the server needs it). Tar behaviour is unchanged.
- Needs a rebuild of the DLL. NOT built or tested in game (no compiler / game in the authoring session). `[General] DebugLog = true` logs `Strainer ... locked to Tar/Water`.

## 0.7.0
- New: `[Visuals] HoseStyle` drop-down (`Transparent` / `Solid`) replaces the `ClearHoses` true/false switch. An old `ClearHoses = false` is carried over once as `Solid` (ConfigVersion 3). `HoseOpacity` only applies to `Transparent`. Read at game start: restart after changing it.
- New: walls hold the tar. The pool made from pumped tar measured its ground with the vanilla pit's ray mask (terrain only), so built pieces were invisible to it and the tar could not stand against them. Read from the IL of `LiquidVolume.UpdateHeights`: the mask is the volume's `m_groundLayer`. Instead of changing that mask (it would also catch the hoses over the pool), a postfix on `UpdateHeights` (`TarPits.ApplyWalls`, pump pools only) raises every cell where a built piece stands on the ground / on another piece to the top of that piece (0.2 m sphere cast, layers piece / static_solid / Default / Default_small, triggers ignored). Pieces of this mod, players, items and anything floating over the pool (a roof) are ignored; tar that stood where a wall appears is pushed to the open cells next to it. Walls that are removed drop out again on the next height pass (about 1 s). `[Drain] PoolWalls` (default true).
- New: overflow. `FillBasin` now fills basin after basin (`FloodPass`, up to 4): the level rises in the walled basin up to the lowest point of its rim; when more tar arrives than fits, the rest runs over that point and fills whatever lies behind it (a film thinner than 4 cm is not kept), anything that finds no basin runs off at the edge of the grid. Before, the ground behind the rim was filled up to the level of the pool, which put a raised column of tar outside the wall. `[Drain] PoolOverflow` (default true; off = old behaviour).
- Version 0.7.0 (new minor: everyone on the server needs it).
- Needs a rebuild of the DLL. NOT built or tested in game (no compiler / game in the authoring session). `[General] DebugLog = true` logs `N cells raised by built pieces` and `x of y m3 stayed in the basin`. If the tar still does not stand against a wall, send that log.
